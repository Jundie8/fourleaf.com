Privacy Policy

Last updated 18 November 2026

Greenlit reads restaurant menus and sorts the dishes against your diet. The personal part, your diets, allergies and the foods you avoid, is worked out on your phone and never sent to us. This page explains exactly what does leave your device, and why.

The short version.

  • Your diets, allergies, food preferences, scan history and the people you add to your table stay on your phone. We never receive them.
  • iCloud Sync is optional and off until you turn it on. If you do, your profile and table are also saved to your own private iCloud, which we can’t read. Scans and photos are not synced.
  • When you scan a menu, the photos are sent to our server and on to OpenAI so the menu can be read. We don’t keep the photos.
  • If you agree when the app first asks, we collect basic usage and crash data, tied to a random ID on your device rather than to your name. It is off until you say yes, and you can change your mind in Settings. There are no accounts.
  • Subscriptions are bought through Apple. We never see your payment details.
  • No ads, no tracking across other companies’ apps or websites, and we don’t sell your data.

Who we are

Greenlit is made by Four Leaf (“we”, “us”). For any privacy question, or to use a right described below, write to support@tryfourleaf.com.

Greenlit has a free plan and an optional paid subscription, which this page calls Plus.

What stays on your device

This is stored on your iPhone and is not sent to us or anyone else:

  • Your diet profile: the diets you follow, the allergies you flag, your avoid list, and the foods you mark as loved or disliked.
  • The people at your table: if you add the people you eat with (up to five besides you), their names, diets, allergies and avoid lists, so Greenlit can check a menu for the whole table. You can remove anyone at any time.
  • Your scan history: the menus you’ve scanned, kept until you clear them (up to 50 menus), and your running totals.
  • Menu photos: the photos behind your recent scans are kept on your phone for up to 28 days, for your 20 most recent scans, so you can check a menu against the original. With Plus they are kept for up to a year, for your 50 most recent scans. If Plus ends, photos older than 28 days, and any beyond your 20 most recent scans, are deleted the next time the app opens or saves a scan. They are excluded from iCloud backups.
  • Your settings, such as appearance and whether iCloud Sync is on.
  • A count of this month’s scans, which the app uses to apply the free plan’s monthly scan allowance.
  • A random install ID and a queue of usage events waiting to be sent (see below).

An allergy list says something about your body. That is why Greenlit works the way it does: the app decides which dishes suit you by running rules on your phone against the menu text, rather than sending your profile to a server. The same goes for the people you add to your table. You decide what to save about them, so add only what they’re comfortable with.

You can delete this data yourself. Settings → Clear Scan History removes saved menus and menu photos immediately. Settings → Reset Profile clears your diets, allergies and preferences and removes the people at your table; if iCloud Sync is on, it also asks iCloud to delete your copy, which needs an internet connection (see iCloud Sync below). To remove one person, use Profile → Settings → People at my table. Deleting the app removes everything stored on the device, but not an iCloud copy.

What leaves your device

Menu photos

When you scan a menu, the photos are sent over an encrypted connection to our scanning service, which forwards them to OpenAI to be read and turned into structured menu data. Photos you share into Greenlit from other apps are handled the same way. Before upload, each photo is resized and re-encoded, which removes metadata such as the time and location a photo was taken.

  • The request contains the photos only. It does not contain your diets, allergies, preferences, name, email or location.
  • We don’t store the photos. The extracted menu goes back to your phone and isn’t saved on our servers. We only log that a scan happened, as described under usage data below.
  • OpenAI handles what it receives under its own API terms and data controls (see its data usage page). OpenAI keeps API inputs, including menu photos, for up to 30 days to detect abuse, and may keep them longer where the law requires it or to prevent harm. OpenAI states that data sent through its API is not used to train its models by default.

Usage and crash data

If you agree, the app sends basic events to our own database, hosted by Supabase: which screens are opened, which buttons are tapped, when the app is opened or sent to the background, whether a scan completed or failed (and the kind of failure, and how many dishes it found), whether a scan was turned away by the hourly scan limit, whether you matched a menu to a restaurant (not which one), where the Plus paywall was opened from, which plan you chose and how a purchase or restore ended (never the price or your payment details), whether you turned iCloud Sync on or off, and crash diagnostics delivered by iOS. Each event carries your iPhone model, iOS version, app version, a random session ID, and a random install ID.

  • The install ID is a random code generated the first time you open the app. It isn’t linked to your name, email, Apple ID or advertising identifier. Deleting the app removes it, and a fresh install creates a new one. The app shows it under Settings → Privacy & Support → Device ID (tap to copy), which is what to quote if you ask us to delete data.
  • We use these events to keep the app reliable, fix bugs, and decide what to build. We use no third-party analytics or advertising service.
  • These events never carry your diets, allergies or food choices, or the names or diets of anyone at your table.
  • It is off until you agree. The first time you open the app after setup it asks once whether to share usage and crash data. Until you say yes, no usage or crash event is sent. Settings → Privacy & Support → Share Usage Data changes your answer at any time; switching it off stops every usage and crash event from being sent, and discards any still waiting on your phone. Switching it off doesn’t remove events we have already received; see Your choices and rights.
  • Saying no, or turning it off, doesn’t stop the install ID travelling with each scan. The scanning service uses it to apply the hourly scan limit, as described under Your IP address below.

iCloud Sync (optional)

Plus includes an iCloud Sync switch (Profile → Settings → iCloud Sync). It is off until you turn it on. When it is on, the app saves your diet profile (diets, allergies, avoid list, diet exceptions and the foods you’ve rated) and the people at your table (their names and the same details) to your own private iCloud, so they follow you to your other devices signed in to the same Apple Account. It syncs when the app opens, when it returns to the foreground, and shortly after you change something.

  • This data goes from your phone to Apple’s iCloud, not to us, and we have no access to it. It counts towards your iCloud storage, and Apple handles it under its privacy policy.
  • Scan history and menu photos are not synced.
  • Each saved item carries the time it was last changed. Removing a person leaves a marker without their name, so the removal reaches your other devices.
  • Turning the switch off stops syncing but leaves the copy that is already in your iCloud. Reset Profile, while sync is on, turns sync off and asks iCloud to delete that copy. This needs an internet connection, so if you reset while offline the copy stays. Deleting the app leaves the copy in place, and so does the end of your Plus subscription, which also stops syncing. To delete a copy yourself, open your iPhone’s Settings, tap your name, then iCloud, then Manage Account Storage, choose the app and delete its data.

Subscriptions

Plus is bought through your Apple Account. Apple takes the payment and manages billing, renewals and cancellation. We don’t receive your card or billing details, your name or your Apple Account. The app asks Apple, on your phone, whether your subscription is active, and it doesn’t send Apple’s receipts or transaction IDs to us. If you’ve agreed to share usage data, we do see that a purchase was started and how it ended, as described under Usage and crash data.

Restaurant name search

When you name a menu or look for a restaurant, the text you type may be sent to Apple’s Maps search to suggest matching places. We don’t send your location, and the app doesn’t request it. Apple handles that search under its privacy policy.

Your IP address

Our scanning service sees the IP address your request comes from, as any internet service does. We use it to enforce a limit on scans per hour, so the service can’t be run up by abuse. For this the service keeps two counters, one for your install ID and one for the IP address (so that one network can’t be used to get around the per-install limit). Each is only a number and a time. Routine cleanup removes a counter once it has been idle for more than two hours; the cleanup runs when the service next handles a scan, so in a quiet period a counter can stay longer. The IP address isn’t joined to your scans or your usage events, and it isn’t shared. Our hosting provider may also keep its own standard infrastructure logs.

What we don’t do

  • No accounts. This version doesn’t ask you to sign in, and we collect no name or email address. iCloud Sync and purchases use the Apple Account already on your phone, which we never see.
  • No location. The app doesn’t request or use your location.
  • No advertising and no cross-app tracking. There is no ad network or attribution service in the app. Nothing about you is combined with data from other companies’ apps or websites, so the app doesn’t ask for App Tracking Transparency permission.
  • No sale or sharing. We don’t sell your information or share it for cross-context behavioural advertising, as those terms are used in California law, and we don’t sell data to brokers.

How long things are kept

  • Menu photos on our servers: not retained. OpenAI keeps them for up to 30 days, as described above.
  • Menu photos on your phone: up to 28 days, and for your 20 most recent scans; with Plus, up to a year, and for your 50 most recent scans.
  • Your iCloud copy, if you use iCloud Sync: until it is deleted as described under iCloud Sync. We have no access to it, so we can’t delete it for you.
  • Usage and crash events: kept for as long as they help us understand and improve the app. We don’t yet apply an automatic deletion schedule.
  • Scan-limit counters (including IP addresses): removed once idle for more than two hours, at the next routine cleanup (see Your IP address above).
  • On your device: as described above, or until you delete them or the app.

Your choices and rights

  • Delete local data with Clear Scan History and Reset Profile, or by deleting the app.
  • Turn usage and crash data on or off with Settings → Privacy & Support → Share Usage Data.
  • Turn iCloud Sync on or off with Profile → Settings → iCloud Sync, and remove people from your table with Profile → Settings → People at my table.
  • Manage or cancel Plus in your Apple Account’s subscription settings. Apple handles billing and refunds, not us.
  • Revoke camera or photo access any time in iOS Settings. The app uses them only to capture or select menu photos.
  • Access, correction, deletion. Depending on where you live, you may have rights over your personal information, for example under the GDPR or UK GDPR (access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability, objection) or under California and other US state privacy laws. We hold no name or email for you, so we can usually act on data only if it can be tied to your install ID, which the app shows under Settings → Privacy & Support → Device ID. Write to support@tryfourleaf.com with that ID and we’ll help where we reasonably can, and respond within 30 days. Usage events tied to an install ID are deleted on request.
  • Where the GDPR applies, our legal basis for reading menu photos is performance of the service you asked for, and for usage and crash events it is your consent, which you can withdraw at any time in Settings, and for scan limiting it is our legitimate interest in keeping the app reliable and affordable to run. You may object to the latter, and you may complain to your local data protection authority.

Children

Greenlit is not directed to children under 13, and we don’t knowingly collect personal information from them. You can add family members, including children, to your table. Those details stay on your phone (and in your own iCloud if you turn on sync) and are not sent to us.

International transfers

Our service providers process data in the United States. If you use the app elsewhere, the menu photos you scan and your usage events will be processed there. If you turn on iCloud Sync, where Apple stores your copy is governed by Apple’s terms, not ours.

Security

Traffic between the app and our service is encrypted in transit. Access to our servers is restricted, and the app can add usage events to our analytics table but cannot read or change what is already stored in it. Your iCloud copy is protected by your Apple Account and Apple’s iCloud security, and we have no access to it.

Changes to this policy

If we change what the app collects, we’ll update this page and the date above before the change ships. Material changes, such as adding accounts or collecting location, will be described here explicitly.
